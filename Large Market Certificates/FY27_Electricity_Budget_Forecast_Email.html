Below is the FY27 electricity emissions forecast for the budget, on the market-based method, by business unit. I have split T&I into Australia and New Zealand because the two are on different renewable arrangements and are moving in opposite directions at the moment. The last row is the total market-based figure.

The numbers come from the Envizi electricity export taken 6 Sep 26, Operational Control accounts only. June is the last month before the renewal agreements started; July and August are the first two months under them. The FY27 column is the July–August average multiplied by 12, so it is a run-rate rather than a model, and the notes under the table say what will move it.

Market-based = Scope 2 + Other CO2e as reported by Envizi; the certificate credits sit in Other. Brackets are credits. Figures are rounded to the tonne, so the sub-rows may not add exactly.

What the table is telling us

  • T&I Australia has come down from about 1,274 t a month to about 214 t. 53 certificate accounts are now crediting against the large market sites (there were 2 before July). 17 permanent accounts and 9 temporary ones are still to be built, so the run-rate will fall further once those are in.
  • T&I New Zealand has gone the other way, from about 34 t to about 119 t a month. This is not real. The 21 Ecotricity certificate accounts are still mirroring their kWh, but the NZ REC factor (RECs NZ - 2026) has no value from 1 July, so the credit that was worth about 93 t in June is 0 t in July and August. Category Management confirmed all NZ electricity is renewable from 1 Jul 26 (TOU on Ecotricity now, NTOU from 1 Jan 27), so once a FY27 NZ factor is loaded and the NTOU sites are set up, the NZ figure should be close to zero. For the budget I would carry NZ at the June level or lower, not the July run-rate.
  • The July and August Australian figures are still overstated. Fourteen old retailer accounts are accruing alongside the Engie accounts that replaced them (about 367 t a month across the two months, mostly T&I asphalt sites), and they carry no certificate credit. They are being closed off at 30 Jun 26, which takes that out.
  • Two things go the other way. Most Australian certificate accounts are on 24-25 LGC factors against 25-26 electricity factors, so they over-credit by about 3% until the 25-26 factors are loaded, and the Maryborough account in RTS copies the gross NMI figure while the CQMS foundry deduction nets the location, which over-credits RTS by about 95 t a month until the gross-or-net question is settled.
  • NT and the named rail exclusions stay on grid factors under the agreements, so they are the floor for T&I Australia and RTS.

Net of all that, I would budget T&I Australia and RTS somewhat below the run-rate in the table, NZ well below it, and EU, SICS and Corporate at about the run-rate. I will refresh the table once the remaining accounts are built and the NZ factor is in, which should be the October data.
